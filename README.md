Popularised by Nigel Kneale's 1972 BBC television play The Stone Tape; the underlying idea has older roots in 19th- and 20th-century parapsychology.

## Overview

The 'Stone Tape theory' is a parapsychological hypothesis that buildings, especially old stone buildings, can store imprints of intense emotional or traumatic past events much as a magnetic tape stores audio, and that under suitable conditions sensitive observers can perceive these recordings as visual, auditory, or affective hauntings. The name and modern popular form are owed to Nigel Kneale's BBC play The Stone Tape, broadcast on 25 December 1972, in which a research team investigates a haunted Victorian mansion as an apparent recording medium. Kneale's framing drew on older ideas: Charles Babbage's 1837 speculation that sound permanently impresses itself on the air; Edmund Gurney and Eleanor Sidgwick's 19th-century Society for Psychical Research speculations; H. H. Price's 1940s 'psychic ether' hypothesis; and especially T. C. Lethbridge's 1961 book Ghost and Ghoul, which proposed that emotional energy is stored in the local energy field of natural features. The 'Stone Tape' label is now applied across paranormal media to so-called residual hauntings, in which an apparition repeats the same actions in the same location regardless of observers. Mainstream scientists reject the theory as having no plausible physical mechanism; the geologist and skeptic Sharon Hill has argued that the term itself misleadingly suggests scientific credibility for a speculation lacking any physical basis.

## Etymology

- **Origin**: Coined by Nigel Kneale for his 1972 BBC television play The Stone Tape; the play's hypothesis was rapidly adopted by paranormal writers as the 'Stone Tape theory'.
- **Meaning**: Analogy between the storage of past events in stone or masonry and the magnetic storage of audio on a recording tape.
- **First use**: 1972 (Kneale, The Stone Tape, BBC)

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Residual recording in stone or matter** _(Nigel Kneale (dramatist), T. C. Lethbridge, Various paranormal investigators)_

Strong emotional events imprint onto the surrounding stone, masonry, or other materials and replay automatically under triggering conditions, perceived by sensitive observers as a haunting.

**Psychic ether** _(H. H. Price)_

An ether-like medium permeates space and stores emotional impressions, retrievable by sensitive observers.

### Skeptical

**No physical mechanism** _(Sharon Hill, Theodore Schick, Lewis Vaughn, Benjamin Radford)_

No known property of stone, brick, or any other building material allows it to store complex spatio-temporal scenes of past events, let alone replay them; the analogy to magnetic tape is purely rhetorical.

**Pareidolia, expectation, and cultural transmission** _(Richard Wiseman, Christopher French)_

Reports of residual hauntings are best explained by perceptual pareidolia, prior knowledge of a site's reputation, suggestion, and the cultural transmission of ghost narratives.
